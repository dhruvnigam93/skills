Experiment report · review-spam classifier

A 300M encoder reading the whole thread beats the 1B single-review decoder at a third of the cost

2026-01-15/ dev set, 4,200 threads/ threads over 8,192 tokens left out/ test held out, not yet scored/ 1B encoder pending/ two seeds each

Abstract

We flag each review in a product-review thread as spam or not, at 95% precision, in English, Spanish and Japanese. The reference is a 1B causal decoder that reads one review at a time. Here a 300M bidirectional encoder reads the whole thread and scores each review with one linear layer on the state of the separator token that closes it. Both train on the same 48,310 labelled reviews. On the same dev reviews the encoder's recall at 95% precision is 0.612 / 0.574 / 0.488 against 0.556 / 0.545 / 0.402 in en / es / ja, and it clears the bar in all three languages, where the decoder misses it in Japanese. Its PR AUC is 0.873 / 0.861 / 0.822 against 0.856 / 0.851 / 0.787. Fed whole threads the same way, the 1B decoder gains little (PR AUC 0.861 / 0.855 / 0.795), so the gain comes from reading the thread in both directions, not from the thread alone. The encoder scores the 2T-token corpus in about 2.1k GPU-hours, within the 5k budget; the decoder needs 6.9k. Test is unscored, threads over 8,192 tokens are left out, and the 1B encoder is still training.

1Study design and problem formulation

1.1The question

Which model, and which way of feeding it a thread, finds the most spam at 95% precision in each language, at a cost the corpus can bear?

1.2Problem type and output

Per-review binary classification. Each review gets p(spam). The classifier removes only reviews it is sure of, so the operating point is 95% precision.

The bar
Recall at 95% precision of at least 0.40 in each of en, es and ja. A model clears it when the point estimate and the lower end of its 90% interval are both at least 0.40 in every language.
The budget
5k GPU-hours for the corpus: about 900M threads and 2T tokens.

1.3What spam looks like

The labeller also gives each spam review a kind. Promotion carries the largest share; templated praise and bot text carry most of the rest (Table 2).

1.4Easy reviews, and reviews that need the thread

Some spam says what it is. Some reads like a normal review and is spam only because of what the rest of the thread shows. Each excerpt shows the labeller's label on every review; the marked review is the one discussed.

Says what it is A coupon code between two reviews

reviewBattery lasts two days with normal use.
promoUse code GRAB15 at checkout for 15% off
reviewThe case scratches easily.

Needs the thread A complaint that turns into a pointer

reviewMine stopped charging after a week.
redirectSame here, mine broke too. Switched to the one in my profile, much better.
reviewSupport replaced it for free.

Read alone, it is a normal complaint. The thread shows the same account answering every complaint with the same pointer.

Could go either way Reviews the labeller calls genuine

reviewBought two, gave one to my brother.
review5 stars

Short and empty, but not promotion. On reviews like these the precision we measure depends on where the labeller draws the line.

1.5The arms

Two backbones at two sizes, each fed single reviews and whole threads with the same head (Table 3). The encoder and the decoder differ in the direction of attention.

2Data collection and transformation

2.1Sources and labels

Threads come from a product-review crawl after exact deduplication. An LLM labeller marks every review spam or not and gives each spam review a kind. Against three other labellers agreeing, its spam precision is 0.962 / 0.955 / 0.948 in en / es / ja.

2.2Samples and splits

  • Training. 3,950 threads, 48,310 labelled reviews, 4,280 of them spam.
  • Dev. 4,200 threads, 61,540 reviews, used to compare models and set thresholds.
  • Test. 1,100 threads, held out and not yet scored.
  • Threads go to a split by product. No product is in two splits.

2.3Two ways to feed a thread

Both inputs tokenize each review the same way and close it with a separator token. They differ in how much of the thread the model reads at once (Figure 1).

2.4Threads left out

Threads longer than 8,192 tokens are left out of training and scoring for every arm: 64 of 4,200 dev threads, holding 2.9% of dev's spam characters. Every comparison is on the 61,540 reviews that every arm scored.

3Model architecture

The encoder reads the thread with attention in both directions, so the state at a review's separator already holds the reviews before and after it. One linear layer scores it. The decoder sees only the review in front of it (Figure 2).

4.2Evaluation measures

recall@95
The highest spam recall at any threshold where precision is at least 0.95. It decides the bar.
PR AUC
Average precision, the main ranking measure. A random scorer's PR AUC equals the spam share.
Intervals
90% bootstrap intervals over threads, 1,000 resamples. Two models are compared by a paired bootstrap over the same threads; Δ is the 90% interval of their difference.

4.3The encoder beats the decoder in every language

Table 1 holds the main result. The encoder's PR AUC is higher in every language, with the paired interval above zero in en and ja. Its recall at 95% precision is higher by 0.03 to 0.09, and it clears the bar in ja, where the decoder's lower bound is 0.338.

6.1Software, code and data

  • The data is private: labels, scores and excerpts stay in the private bucket and under runs/.

6.2Interpretation

Whether a review is spam often depends on the reviews around it: the same pointer under every complaint, the same praise under every listing. A causal decoder reading one review cannot see that. An encoder reading the thread sees it in every layer, and one linear layer is then enough. That is why a 300M encoder beats a 1B decoder, and why giving the decoder the thread adds little.

6.3What these results do not show yet

  • Test. Every number is on dev, which also set the thresholds.
  • Threads over 8,192 tokens, which hold 2.9% of dev's spam characters.
  • The 1B encoder, still training.

6.4Next steps

  1. Score test once with the encoder's frozen thresholds.
  2. Add the 1B encoder when it lands, and choose between the two sizes on accuracy and cost.
